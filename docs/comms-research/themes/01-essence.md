# 01 — The essence

The single hardest, most valuable thing: say what this is in one true sentence
that a non-technical organiser feels in their gut. Everything else is built from
this.

## The core sentence (candidates — to be tested)

> **Brehon Consensus is a fair, open way for any group to govern itself —
> deciding together who belongs and what's allowed, in the open, by consent, and
> with repair instead of punishment.**

Tighter:

> **A toolkit for groups to make their own rules and enforce them fairly,
> together — no bosses, no secret moderation, no permanent exile.**

Tightest (the 7-word version):

> **Fair self-government for any online community.**

## The two jobs (founder's locked framing, 2026-06-27)

The product does exactly two things, named in warm human words:

> **Membership & Mediation** — who belongs, and how things get put right.

- **Membership** = who's in, how trust is built and earned.
- **Mediation** = handling conflict by a fair peer process aimed at *repair*
  (what others call "moderation", done restoratively).

"Self-**governance**" is the abstract umbrella for both — used for press and
category framing, never as the greeting word. See
[`08-the-word-for-what-it-does.md`](08-the-word-for-what-it-does.md).

## The three pillars (the whole story hangs on these)

Everything the product does ladders up to exactly three promises. Use these as
the section headers of the pamphlet.

### 1. You make your own rules
The group writes its own rules and can change them. Nobody hands them down from
above. *(Customisable rule sets. The "self-organise" promise.)*

### 2. You decide together, in the open
Serious decisions are made by ordinary members drawn like a jury — not by
admins — and every decision is written down with its reasons, where everyone can
see. *(Juries, consensus thresholds, public case log, transparency.)*

### 3. You repair, you don't just punish
When something goes wrong the response starts gentle and aims to fix the harm
and bring people back. Banishment is the last resort, never the first.
*(Graduated restorative sanctions, reintegration, no permanent outcasts.)*

> Make · Decide · Repair — three words, in order. A reader who remembers only
> these three has the whole thing.

## Why it's trustworthy (the proof beneath the promise)

Two supports, used only when the reader asks "but does this actually work?":

- **It's been done before, many times.** Humans have run fair, rule-based,
  consent-based societies throughout history — Brehon law (early Ireland) is one
  well-documented example among many worldwide. This isn't utopian invention;
  it's recovered practice. (See [`03-anti-tina.md`](03-anti-tina.md).)
- **Trust is relational, and risk is shared.** You get in because people vouch
  for you, and they stake their own standing on you. That single mechanic is
  what makes it resistant to fake accounts, brigading, and clique-capture — the
  things that wreck ordinary online groups.

## The one-line takeaway (already in the repo, keep it)

> **Trust is not given by the system — it is extended by other people, and risk
> is shared.**

This is excellent and already battle-tested in the docs. It's the *philosophy*
line. Pair it with the *benefit* line ("make your own rules, fairly") — benefit
leads, philosophy supports.

## What the essence must NOT do

- Must not lead with "Lemmy fork" or any tech name — that's *how*, not *what*.
- Must not lead with "ancient Irish law" — that's the *proof*, not the *pitch*.
- Must not lead with blockchain — it's a maybe-someday footnote and it misleads.
- Must not promise utopia — it governs *membership and moderation*, nothing more.
  Narrow and honest beats grand and vague.
