# 08 — The word for what it does: moderation / mediation / governance

Founder raised all three, across messages. This is the *activity-level* naming
question (file 02 is the *thing-level* one). It matters as much, because it's the
verb the reader uses to understand "what does it actually handle?"

## The three words, honestly compared

| Word | What it gets right | What it gets wrong | Reader temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moderation** | The word people *search for* and recognise. Names the pain ("our moderation is a mess"). | Implies someone-with-power-deletes-things. Punitive, top-down — the very thing we're replacing. | Warm/familiar, but wrong-spirited |
| **Mediation** | Captures the *spirit* — fair process, repair, peers resolving conflict. | Narrower than reality (we also handle *membership* and rule-making, not just disputes). Less searched. | Warm, right-spirited, slightly narrow |
| **Governance** | Most *accurate*. Covers all of it: membership + rules + decisions + disputes. The product genuinely IS a governance layer. | Cold, abstract, "political-science-y." A community-garden organiser doesn't say "we need governance." | Cool/technical, precise |

## ★ FOUNDER DECISION (2026-06-27)

> **"Membership & Mediation"** is the product's two-job framing — both warm,
> human words, and they sit beautifully side by side (and alliterate).
> **"Governance"** stays in play but **only at the high, abstract level** — the
> umbrella category for press, the about-page, the GitHub one-liner. It is *not*
> the greeting word.

So the locked hierarchy is:
- **Greeting / pamphlet:** *Membership & Mediation* (the two jobs, in human words)
- **Spirit/contrast:** moderation → *mediation* (the reveal that it's not punitive)
- **Abstract umbrella:** *self-governance* (press, category, "what shelf")

Everything below is the reasoning that led here; keep for the copywriter.

## The resolution: layer them by audience depth (same pattern as file 02)

Don't pick one and ban the others. Use the **right word for the right reader**,
and let the warm word open the door to the precise one.

- **Door (organiser, pamphlet front):** the pain-word they already use.
  > "Every group hits the same wall: deciding who's in, agreeing the rules, and
  > handling it when someone breaks them."
  Name the *jobs*, not the *category*. No -tion word needed yet.

- **The reveal (one level in):** the spirit-word as the differentiator.
  > "Most tools call this **moderation** and hand it to admins. We treat it more
  > like **mediation** — fair, by peers, aimed at repair."

- **The accurate name (press / contributors / about page):** the precise word.
  > "Taken together, it's a **self-governance** layer: membership, rule-making,
  > and dispute resolution, run by the community itself."

> **Sequence:** name the *jobs* → contrast moderation/mediation → land on
> *governance* as the umbrella. "Governance" is the destination word, not the
> greeting word.

## Why "governance" wins as the umbrella (but not the greeting)

The product does three things — **membership, rule-making, dispute-handling** —
and only "governance" covers all three honestly. "Moderation" and "mediation"
both describe just the dispute slice. So for accuracy (file 02's "layer"
question, press copy, the GitHub description), **governance is correct.** It's
just too cold to *lead* with for organisers. Warm the reader up with jobs and
the moderation→mediation pivot first; "governance" then lands as clarifying, not
alienating.

## One-liner that uses all three honestly

> "It handles what most people call **moderation** — but more like
> **mediation**, and really it's the whole job of a community **governing
> itself**: who belongs, what the rules are, and what happens when they're
> broken."
