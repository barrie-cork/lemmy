# 14 — Bottom-up, not top-down (where rules come from)

Founder point (2026-06-27): In Brehon law, rules were **bottom-up, not
top-down** — they grew from **norms → customs → recognised good practice →
eventually codified as law.** Rules emerged from how people actually lived and
what worked, *then* got written down. This may be the single cleanest organising
idea for the whole pitch.

## The core claim

> **Most platforms are top-down: the rules, the moderation, and the power all
> flow down from whoever owns the site. Brehon Consensus is bottom-up: rules grow
> up from your community's own practice and consent — the way Brehon law itself
> did.**

## The Brehon mechanism (the historical backbone)

Brehon law wasn't handed down by a king or a state. It rose from the ground:

```
norms  →  customs  →  recognised good practice  →  codified as law
```

A rule got written down *because it had already proven itself in practice* — not
before. Codification was the **last** step, not the first. That's the opposite of
how we usually imagine law (a legislature decrees, everyone obeys).

This is the real backbone for everything the product claims about
self-governance: it's not a novel invention, it's a **recovered way of making
rules** that actually functioned for centuries.

## How it maps onto the product (the same loop, today)

The corrected moderation flow ([`13`](13-the-moderation-journey.md)) *is* the
bottom-up loop running in software:

```
a felt need  →  someone proposes a rule  →  members decide by consent
            →  it becomes part of how the community governs
            →  cases are judged against it
```

Rules grow **from** the community, not **onto** it. You can only be sanctioned
against norms the members themselves raised and agreed. That's what makes the
rules *legitimate* rather than imposed.

## Consensus method is itself bottom-up (experimentation)

There's **no single mandated way** to adopt a rule. Each community uses **whatever
consensus method suits it** — and figuring out what works *is part of the point.*
This ties straight to *The Dawn of Everything* (see
[`03-anti-tina.md`](03-anti-tina.md)): **experimentation is key.** Small
communities trying different rule-sets and decision methods *is* the alternative
in action — not a bug to be standardised away.

## The "bottom-up at every layer" through-line

This is the candidate spine for the whole pamphlet — it unifies almost every
theme:

| Layer | Top-down (mainstream platforms) | Bottom-up (Brehon Consensus) |
|---|---|---|
| **Rules** | handed down by the owner/admin | grow from members' practice + consent (this file) |
| **Moderation** | a moderator class / opaque T&S team | shared across everyone, jury-drawn ([`12`](12-everybody-moderates.md)) |
| **Reputation** | algorithm scores you from above | earned by helping your peers ([`10`](10-reputation-is-earned-by-helping.md)) |
| **Membership** | platform lets anyone in / bans at will | members vouch each other in ([`11`](11-invitation-as-defence.md)) |
| **Stakes** | set by the platform / hidden | set by the community, by consent ([`10`](10-reputation-is-earned-by-helping.md)) |
| **Power** | flows down from who owns the site | flows up from the membership |

> One-liner candidate for the whole project: **"Bottom-up, not top-down — at
> every layer."**

## Tone guard-rails

- "Bottom-up" is intuitive and warm — use it. But don't let it imply *no
  structure / chaos.* Bottom-up here means **rules still exist and are clear** —
  they just *originate* with the members. Order from consent, not from a boss.
- Keep the Brehon backbone **sober** (per [`03`](03-anti-tina.md)): it's evidence
  that bottom-up rule-making *worked*, not romance.
