# 07 — Traps & tone

What will quietly wreck this messaging if we're not deliberate. Written as
guard-rails the pamphlet/site copy must pass.

## Word choices that change the whole meaning

### "Moderation" vs "Mediation"  ★ founder raised this — important
The founder's instinct is right and worth getting exactly right.

- **"Moderation"** is the *honest, searchable category*. It's what people call
  the problem ("Reddit moderation is broken", "Facebook moderators"). It's how
  organisers will search for a solution. We can't abandon it — it's the door
  people knock on.
- **"Mediation"** is the *truer description of the spirit*. Moderation =
  someone-with-power-removes-stuff. Mediation = a fair process helps people
  resolve a conflict and repair it. The restorative, consensus, jury-of-peers
  model genuinely *is* closer to mediation than to moderation.

**Recommendation: use both, deliberately, in this shape —**
> "It handles the thing every group struggles with — what people call
> *moderation* — but it does it more like **mediation**: a fair process, decided
> by peers, that aims to repair rather than punish."

Lead with "moderation" to be *found* and *understood*; pivot to "mediation" to
reveal the *difference*. The pivot IS the pitch — it's the moment the reader
realises this isn't more-of-the-same. Consider it a recurring rhetorical move,
not a one-off.

## The five tone traps

### 1. Jargon creep
Federation, ActivityPub, reputation event-sourcing, quorum, sybil, hash chain —
all real, all death to a grassroots reader. **Rule:** if a 15-year-old organiser
wouldn't say it, it doesn't go in the pamphlet. Technical terms live in the
contributor docs, not the leaflet.

### 2. Ancient-Celtic romance
"Mystical wisdom of the ancient Irish" makes it sound like a theme park and
invites "is this a nationalist thing?" Brehon is *evidence that alternatives
exist*, presented soberly — not heritage cosplay. (See
[`03-anti-tina.md`](03-anti-tina.md) tone guard-rails.)

### 3. Utopia-creep / over-claiming
The system governs **membership and moderation. Full stop.** It will not fix
capitalism, end injustice, or build the new world on its own. Over-claiming
destroys trust with exactly the savvy organisers we want. **Narrow + honest >
grand + vague.** Let the small true claim feel big, rather than making a big
claim feel hollow.

### 4. Tech-lead framing
"A Lemmy fork in Rust with an Extism plugin host" — true, irrelevant to
organisers, and it buries the lede. Tech is *how*; lead with *what* and *why*.
Tech specs belong in the r/rust post and CONTRIBUTING.md, not here.

### 5. "Reputation" misreads (social-credit / clout)
"Reputation" makes savvy readers hear **social-credit surveillance** or
**influencer clout**. Both are the opposite of this. The fix is positive, not a
disclaimer: standing is **earned by helping** the community govern itself, and
**posting/liking/sharing are irrelevant to it** — engagement is not reputation.
Lead with "trust you earn by helping", never "your score." Full theme:
[`10-reputation-is-earned-by-helping.md`](10-reputation-is-earned-by-helping.md).

### 6. Punitive misread ("is this cancel culture?")
Any mention of juries/consequences risks reading as a punishment machine. Pair
every such mention with the restorative outcome — repair before exile, a way
back. "Accountability without annihilation." Full theme:
[`09-anti-cancel-culture.md`](09-anti-cancel-culture.md).

### 7. Blockchain confusion
The repo is explicit: **no tokens, no on-chain voting, blockchain is a
maybe-someday narrow audit anchor (V3).** If "blockchain" appears anywhere near
the lead, readers will assume crypto and either bounce (FOSS/left organisers
allergic to it) or arrive with wrong expectations (crypto crowd). **Keep it out
of public copy entirely** unless a technical reader specifically asks.

## The honesty line (status)

Real status: alpha, solo dev, v0 = 11 endpoints, Lemmy-only so far, full
governance not all live, cross-protocol is direction not shipped. Copy should
**inspire with the vision** but **never claim the future in the present tense.**
Use "designed to", "will", "the goal is" for unshipped reach. A founder who's
honest about alpha status earns more trust with this audience than one who
oversells — these readers can smell vaporware.

## Tone target (one line)

> Plain, warm, confident, sober. The voice of an experienced organiser who has
> seen groups fall apart over bad process — not a startup, not a manifesto, not a
> historian. Speaks to peers, not to "users."

## The "would I hand this to a friend?" test

Final gate for any pamphlet draft: read it aloud as if handing it to a friend
who runs a community garden's WhatsApp group and is sick of the drama. If a
sentence would make them glaze over, cut it. If a sentence makes them go "oh —
*that'd* actually help us," keep it and build around it.
