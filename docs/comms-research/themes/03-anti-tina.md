# 03 — "There Are Lots Of Alternatives" (the narrative spine)

This is the deepest theme and the founder's strongest idea. It reframes the
whole project from "a moderation tool" to "a piece of a much bigger argument."

## The enemy: TINA

Margaret Thatcher's slogan, now the background hum of neoliberalism:
**"There Is No Alternative"** (TINA). Applied to *how we organise ourselves*, it
says: thin, top-down, representative management is just how things are. Someone's
in charge, the rules come from above, and if you don't like the moderation,
leave. Most people have quietly accepted this is the only realistic option —
online and off.

## The reframe: TALOA — "There Are Lots Of Alternatives"

> **Spine note (founder, 2026-06-27):** anti-TINA is the *narrative* half of the
> pamphlet's spine. Its sharper form — *"someone else has to be in charge" isn't
> a law of nature, it's a choice that was sold to us* — and its mechanism half,
> *bottom-up at every layer*, are unified in
> [`14-bottom-up.md`](14-bottom-up.md) ("the WHY and the HOW"). This file supplies
> the evidence that alternatives are real; theme-14 carries the spine itself.

The counter-argument, and our spine:

> **There are lots of alternatives. Always have been.**

History is not a straight line from "primitive" to "modern representative
democracy." It's a vast, ongoing **experiment**. Humans have organised
themselves countless ways — many of them non-hierarchical, by consent, and yet
fully *rule-based*. They were not chaos. They were not all the same. They were
deliberate, sophisticated, and they worked.

**Lead with the living, not the historical** (research Section B: current
examples build *efficacy* — "a group like yours did this" — where history only
anchors the frame). So in copy, open with groups doing fair self-governance
*now* — mutual-aid networks, worker co-ops, unions, residents' associations —
*then* reach for history as proof it isn't a fluke. History is the backup, not
the argument.

**Key source (for the history backup):** David Graeber & David Wengrow, *The Dawn
of Everything: A New History of Humanity* (2021). Its central claim — humans have
always self-consciously experimented with how to live together, and "stuck in
hierarchy" is a story we tell ourselves, not a fact of nature. ⚠ Solid for the
*general* anti-linear-history argument, but named scholars (Morris, Feinman,
Bell) contest specific archaeological claims — so hedge ("some scholars dispute
the details") and never cite a *specific* Graeber/Wengrow fact as settled.

**Brehon law is one proof among many.** Early Irish Brehon law ran for centuries
with **no state, no police, no prisons** — order came from social trust,
standing (honour-price), sureties, public procedure, and restitution. It is
*one* well-documented, written-down example. Tribal and customary legal
traditions worldwide share the family resemblance: consent, layered belonging,
repair over punishment.

**Customary law has even won in modern courts** — though not *Brehon* law.
(Verified 2026-06-27, research Section E.) Indigenous customary/tribal law has
been recognised as an operative legal source in real cases:

- *Mabo v Queensland* [1992] HCA 23 — Australia's High Court recognised that
  native title, derived from Indigenous customary law, survives colonisation.
- *Ellis v R* [2022] NZSC — New Zealand's Supreme Court held that tikanga Māori
  is "co-constitutive" of the common law.
- *Tsilhqot'in Nation v BC* [2014] SCC 44 — Canada's Supreme Court made the
  first successful Aboriginal-title declaration.

⚠ **These are all First Nations / Indigenous traditions — none Brehon, none
Irish.** There is **no** modern Irish (post-1922) or UK case in which Brehon law
was successfully invoked as binding law. So in public copy: cite these as proof
that *customary law can hold up in a modern court* — never as "Brehon law wins in
court." Brehon law's value here is as a **historical example**, not a live
precedent. The earlier founder recollection of a Brehon-specific case does not
check out and stays OUT.

**And it was bottom-up.** Brehon law didn't descend from a ruler — it grew from
norms → customs → good practice → codified law. That bottom-up origin is itself
the proof that rules *can* come from the community rather than from above, and
it's the historical backbone of the product's whole design. See
[`14-bottom-up.md`](14-bottom-up.md).

## Why this matters for the product

Brehon Consensus is **the alternative made usable**. It takes one of these older,
proven ways of governing — trust, consent, repair, transparent procedure — and
makes it work for any modern online community, in software. It's not nostalgia.
It's not LARPing ancient Ireland. It's: *these patterns worked for a long time;
here's a clean way to run one today.*

## The narrative arc (for a pamphlet / about page)

1. **You've felt it.** Every group you've joined online, someone else made the
   rules and the moderation was a black box. You assumed that's just how it is.
2. **It isn't — look around now.** Co-ops, unions, mutual-aid groups already run
   themselves fairly, by consent and clear rules. (And they're not new: humans
   have done this for thousands of years.)
3. **Here's one, working, for you.** Brehon Consensus lets your group do the
   same: write your own rules, decide together in the open, repair instead of
   punish.
4. **And it's built to travel.** It's *designed to* work across the open tools
   you already use (Lemmy today; more planned).

## Tone guard-rails (critical)

- **Don't romanticise.** No "ancient Celtic wisdom" mysticism. Brehon law had
  slavery and was deeply unequal by modern standards (honour-price scaled by
  rank; unfree classes) — we borrow *structural principles*, not the whole
  society. But (Section B / Lakoff) **don't lead the main narrative with the
  apology** — saying "Brehon had slavery" up front activates the wrong frame.
  Keep it for the FAQ / deep context: "We borrow the structure — consent-based
  rules, repair, public procedure — not Brehon's society." Lead with modern lived
  examples so the historical claim never has to carry the argument.
- **Don't preach.** Anti-TINA is a *door*, not a lecture. Organisers feel the
  problem already — name it once, then show the solution.
- **Don't claim history is a utopia.** The honest claim is *plurality and
  experimentation*, not "the past was better." Graeber/Wengrow's actual point.
- **Verify every historical claim** before it ships. This theme's credibility
  dies on one debunked fact. ✅ Done 2026-06-27 (research Section E): the
  no-state/no-police/no-prisons claim and the bottom-up-origin claim are SOLID
  (Kelly 1988, Stacey 1994, Biggins 2024); the Graeber/Wengrow thesis is solid
  in general but contested on specifics; the Brehon-specific court-case claim is
  FALSE and removed.
