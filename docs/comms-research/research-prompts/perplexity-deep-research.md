# Perplexity Deep Research prompt — evidence-based communication for Brehon Consensus

Paste the block below into **Perplexity** (use **Deep Research** / **Pro** mode
for full source coverage). It is built to return *evidence-based*, *cited*
findings you can act on — not vibes. After it runs, save the output back into
this folder as `perplexity-results-<date>.md` and reconcile it against the theme
files.

> Tip: if the run is too long, split it — run Sections A–C first (the
> communication science), then D–F (positioning + verification) as a second
> prompt that references the first.

---

## THE PROMPT (copy from here ↓)

```
You are a research analyst specialising in evidence-based science communication,
public-interest messaging, and the framing of novel civic-technology products. I
am preparing a leaflet/pamphlet and website for a project called "Brehon
Consensus" and I need a rigorous, well-cited research brief.

CONTEXT — what the project is (do not just repeat this; I need outside evidence
to communicate it well):
- Brehon Consensus is an open-source governance layer for online communities. It
  handles two jobs: MEMBERSHIP (who belongs, how trust is built through vouching)
  and MEDIATION/MODERATION (handling rule-breaking by fair peer juries aimed at
  REPAIR, not punishment).
- Communities write their OWN rules (fully customisable, self-organising).
  Serious decisions are made by randomly-selected member juries, logged publicly
  with reasons. Sanctions are graduated and restorative; reintegration over
  permanent bans.
- It is federated (many independent communities that cooperate but don't rule
  each other) and designed to plug into multiple open protocols (Lemmy/ActivityPub,
  Matrix, PeerTube, MeshCore) — like an open standard for self-governance.
- It is inspired by early Irish Brehon law as ONE example among many historical,
  non-hierarchical, consent-based, rule-based societies. It is NOT crypto, NOT
  blockchain-governance, NOT a token system, NOT a surveillance reputation score.
- Primary audience: NON-TECHNICAL grassroots organisers (mutual aid, co-ops,
  activist groups, unions, community groups).

Produce a structured, heavily-cited research brief covering ALL sections below.
For every claim, cite sources (academic where possible; reputable practitioner
sources otherwise). Flag anything you cannot verify. Prefer specific, actionable
findings over generalities. Use British English.

SECTION A — How to explain a complex/novel idea to non-experts (cognitive science)
- What does research say about ANALOGY and concrete-first explanation for
  comprehension and recall? (e.g. analogical transfer, dual coding, concreteness
  effect.) Give the strongest, most-cited findings and practical rules.
- The "curse of knowledge" and how experts mis-communicate; evidence-based fixes.
- Plain-language / readability research: what measurably improves comprehension
  and trust for low-jargon civic/health/legal communication? (Plain-language
  movement, readability evidence, the cost of jargon.)
- Optimal information chunking and progressive disclosure for leaflets/landing
  pages. Evidence on "message ladders" / layered messaging.

SECTION B — Framing, narrative, and values-based messaging
- Framing theory for social/political communication: what does the evidence say
  about leading with VALUES and BENEFITS vs. features/mechanics? (Lakoff; the
  FrameWorks Institute; Common Cause Foundation values research.)
- Evidence on NARRATIVE and STORY vs. bullet-point facts for persuasion and
  retention (narrative transportation; "story spine").
- How to message an ALTERNATIVE to a dominant status-quo ("There Is No
  Alternative"/TINA) WITHOUT triggering defensiveness or sounding utopian.
  Evidence on counter-narratives, "There Are Lots Of Alternatives", and how
  movements successfully reframe "common sense."
- Loss-aversion vs. hope/agency framing for activating people to ADOPT a new
  practice. What actually drives adoption among community organisers?

SECTION C — Trust, credibility, and avoiding backfire
- For a NEW, alpha-stage, solo-developer project: what builds credibility with
  sceptical, savvy audiences? (Transparency, honesty about limitations, social
  proof, "show the working".)
- Evidence on how OVER-CLAIMING / hype damages trust, especially with
  anti-corporate / activist audiences.
- How to talk about "reputation" and "trust scores" WITHOUT evoking
  surveillance / social-credit dystopia. Any research or case studies on
  reputation-system framing that landed well vs. badly.
- How adjacent projects mishandled crypto/blockchain associations and lost
  audiences — and how to pre-empt that.

SECTION D — Positioning analysis: how comparable projects explain themselves
For EACH of the following, summarise: their one-line pitch, the category NOUN
they use to describe themselves, their lead analogy if any, and what's notably
effective or weak in their public messaging. Quote their actual taglines.
- Mastodon (and the Fediverse / "join the fediverse" messaging)
- Matrix.org
- Nostr
- Loomio (group decision-making)
- Decidim (participatory democracy platform)
- CoBudget / Cobudget, Open Collective
- Consul Democracy, PolicyKit, Metagov (governance tooling)
- Restorative-justice organisations' public explainer language
- Co-operative movement / Platform Cooperativism messaging
Then: extract the PATTERNS — what works repeatedly when explaining
"democratic / governance / federated" tools to non-technical people?

SECTION E — The Brehon / historical-alternatives evidence (VERIFY rigorously)
I want to invoke history as proof that fair, non-hierarchical, rule-based,
consent-based societies have really existed — to counter "there is no
alternative." VERIFY and cite each of these; correct me where I'm wrong:
- David Graeber & David Wengrow, "The Dawn of Everything" (2021): what is their
  ACTUAL central thesis about human social experimentation and non-hierarchy?
  Give accurate, quotable summary + the main scholarly critiques (so I don't
  over-rely on contested claims).
- Early Irish Brehon law: accurate, sober summary of its real features relevant
  here — surety/sureties, honour-price, restitution/restorative justice, absence
  of centralised state enforcement. Cite reputable historical/legal sources.
  ALSO note its problematic aspects (e.g. slavery, hierarchy) so I represent it
  honestly.
- Find a VERIFIABLE modern legal case where customary / tribal / Indigenous law
  was successfully invoked (e.g. Aboriginal Australian native title / Mabo; Māori
  tikanga in NZ courts; Canadian First Nations law; or a genuine Brehon-law
  citation if one exists). Give the real case name, jurisdiction, and outcome. If
  no solid Brehon-specific case exists, say so plainly.
- Other well-documented examples of non-hierarchical or consensus-based
  governance I could cite as "lots of alternatives" (e.g. Iroquois
  Confederacy/Haudenosaunee, Athenian sortition, Quaker consensus, Zapatista
  autonomy, Rojava). For each: one-line accurate description + a caution about
  romanticising.

SECTION F — Naming and the category-noun problem
- This product is hard to categorise (app? system? protocol? layer? "way of
  organising"?). What does positioning/branding research say about naming a
  genuinely NEW category? (Category design, "languaging", the risk of borrowing a
  wrong-category word.)
- How did now-familiar new categories get their nouns ("search engine", "social
  network", "podcast", "wiki")? Lessons for us.
- Evidence on which words convert vs. alienate non-technical civic audiences:
  "governance" vs "moderation" vs "mediation" vs "decision-making" vs
  "self-organising". Any data, A/B tests, or practitioner consensus?

SECTION G — Synthesis & deliverables
- Give me a 1-page synthesis of the TOP 10 evidence-based rules I should follow
  when writing this pamphlet, each with its supporting source.
- Propose 3 distinct positioning angles (with a sample one-liner each) and rank
  them by fit for non-technical grassroots organisers, with reasoning.
- List the 5 biggest messaging RISKS and the evidence-based mitigation for each.
- Provide a short annotated reading list (books, papers, toolkits) for deeper
  follow-up.

FORMAT: Markdown. Use clear headers matching the sections. Cite inline with
links. Put a "⚠ could not verify / contested" note wherever relevant. End with
the Section G synthesis.
```
