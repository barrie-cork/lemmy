## Section A digest — Explaining a novel idea to non-experts → themes/04, 06

- KEY FINDINGS:
  - **Relational structure, not surface similarity, drives analogical transfer** (Gentner 1983; Gick & Holyoak 1980–83). A single analogy transfers spontaneously ~30% of the time; explicitly *naming* it raises to 76%; **two** structurally-similar analogies from different surface domains sharply improve schema abstraction.
  - **Concrete examples before abstract definitions: +40% learning** (Rawson et al. 2015; Pinker). Lead with the vivid scenario, then the definition.
  - **Curse of knowledge is invisible and severe** (Camerer 1989; Newton 1990; Pinker 2014). Newton tapper–listener: tappers predicted 50% comprehension, got 2.5%. Experts wildly over-predict comprehension.
  - **Plain language signals intelligence, not weakness** (Oppenheimer 2006; Shulman 2020). Jargon → lower acceptance, lower perceived intelligence, *increased resistance to persuasion*; functions as social exclusion ("not for you") regardless of glossary.
  - **Working memory holds 3–5 chunks** (Cowan, revising Miller's 7±2). Lists/principle-sets over ~5 items force trade-offs and impede mental-model formation.
  - **Progressive disclosure: +40–124% learnability** (Nielsen). Summary first, details on request, never exceed 2 disclosure levels.
- CONFIRMS: 04 ("every abstract mechanic gets a familiar anchor"; "analogies must be explicit"); 06 (five-length ladder = textbook progressive disclosure; no jargon at low rungs).
- CHALLENGES/CORRECTS:
  - 04 should require **at least two** analogies from different surface domains, not one. (Gick & Holyoak.)
  - 06 rungs 0–1 still lean abstract-benefit-first; harden with a concrete vignette before the benefit. (Rawson.)
  - "Standing earned by helping" needs a **dual-coded visual anchor** (Paivio) — e.g. a concrete image, not prose alone.
- VERIFY FLAGS: Newton (1990) tapper–listener is an unpublished Stanford dissertation; the 2.5%/50% figures are widely repeated but never peer-replicated. Use as illustrative, not precise.
- ACTION ITEMS: 04 → add "use ≥2 analogies" rule. 06 → embed a 2-line concrete vignette before benefit language at rungs 0–1. Both → add "test cold on 3–5 organisers before publishing" callout. 10 → add a dual-coded visual anchor for "standing".

## Section B digest — Framing / narrative / anti-TINA → themes/03

- KEY FINDINGS:
  - **Values > mechanics for grassroots adoption** (Lakoff; FrameWorks). Lead with fairness/voice, never with the algorithm.
  - **Intrinsic values suppress extrinsic ones in the same message** (Common Cause / Schwartz). Mixing "fairness/repair" with "growth/scalability/efficiency" kills both — never in the same breath.
  - **Stories beat statistics for retention** (Heath: 63% recall stories vs 5% stats; narrative-transportation literature). Replace feature lists with one case study.
  - **System-justification defensiveness, not "backfire", is the real barrier** (Jost). Direct attacks on the status quo trigger defence. Invite adoption; show the alternative *enacts existing values better*.
  - **Efficacy-paired hope activates collective action** (Bandura). "A group like yours did this" beats fear-alone, which triggers apathy.
  - **TAPAS/TALA ("There Are Plenty/Lots Of Alternatives") is an established counter-narrative** (Bollier 2014; Narrative Initiative). Brehon embodies it rather than inventing it.
- CONFIRMS: 03 ("There Are Lots Of Alternatives" spine is real and in circulation); bottom-up origin as counter-hegemonic proof (Gramsci); drop tech language.
- CHALLENGES/CORRECTS:
  - **Lead with living examples (mutual aid, co-ops, unions doing fair governance NOW), then historicise.** Current groups build *efficacy*; history only anchors the frame. Reorder theme-03's spine.
  - Tighten Graeber/Wengrow framing — perception risk is real; lead modern, historicise after.
  - "Repair over punishment" needs a concrete dispute→resolution story, not abstract principle.
  - Don't apologise for Brehon's past inequality *in the main narrative* (Lakoff: never use the opponent's frame); handle it in FAQ/deep context only.
- VERIFY FLAGS: "identifiable victim effect" (one named individual = 12 statistics) FAILED preregistered replication (Maier 2023) — don't claim it; the broader stories>stats principle still holds. Lakoff's neural-framing claims are contested as reductionist — use pragmatically. Court-case claim flagged unverified (see Section E).

## Section C digest — Trust / credibility / hype → themes/10, 07

- KEY FINDINGS:
  - **Trustworthiness > expertise for a new/solo project** (Susmann & Wegener 2022). No vested financial interest is a *credibility advantage*; open-code solo dev beats corporate vendor making the same claim.
  - **Pratfall effect** (Aronson 1966): admitting what's not built yet *increases* likability for a perceived-competent source. Savvy audiences hunt for the catch; volunteer it first.
  - **Persuasion-knowledge activation is structural** (Friestad & Wright 1994): "revolutionary", "trust layer", "game-changing" trigger defences in anti-corporate organisers.
  - **Over-claiming trust is worse than not claiming it** (Oliver; expectation-disconfirmation). Failed trust expectations produce durable "why lie?" attribution.
  - **"Reputation system" defaults to social-credit / influencer-clout misreads** (Xu 2023 on framing). Contribution-earned standing is structurally different (Nowak & Sigmund; Resnick) — but you must *say* so.
  - **Crypto association is a contagion hazard in FOSS** (Signal, Mozilla, Wikimedia, SourceHut, Brave cases). Even tiny/optional crypto touches trigger betrayal response. Name it and deny it in historical terms (email, not blockchain).
- CONFIRMS: 10 ("earn standing by helping, not popularity/algorithm" — backed by indirect-reciprocity biology + Stack Overflow≠Reddit-karma evidence; the distinction is structural not rhetorical); 07 (sober organiser-to-peer tone; "show your working"; reputation-misread is a genuine cognitive default).
- CHALLENGES/CORRECTS:
  - 10 "standing decays" — Section C doesn't examine decay; honesty needs a repo check (resolved below: decay is wired but **default-off** in v0).
  - 10 "vouching" dimension is weaker than the others on *observable contribution* — stress-test that copy presents vouching as visible, verifiable peer judgement.
  - 07 blockchain trap → upgrade from "keep it out" to **pre-emptive disassociation**.
- VERIFY FLAGS: Mastodon signed the European Social Stack Open Declaration 2025 (explicit non-crypto anchor). Brave trust-breach (2020 affiliate-link insertion) is real; BAT current perception not examined.
- ACTION ITEMS: 07 → add proactive crypto-disassociation line ("no tokens, no blockchain, no cryptocurrency; decentralisation works like email/post — federated systems that predate crypto by decades"). 10 → verify which "helping" channels are live in v0 (jury live; reporting live; vouching/showing-up — check); consider adopting "earned by helping" as primary term, "reputation" secondary.

## Section C2 digest — Cancel culture / restorative / consent → themes/09, 10

- KEY FINDINGS:
  - **Procedural fairness trumps outcome** (Tyler, 30+ yrs; Thibaut & Walker 1975). People accept unfavourable outcomes far more readily when the *process* was fair (voice, neutrality, respect). Pew 2021/22: 58% see callouts as accountability *when the process feels neutral*.
  - **Graduated sanctions work by preserving dignity** (Ostrom, 800+ cases). Low initial sanction → escalation sustains cooperation; the key is "initial forgiveness", enabling "a way back".
  - **Endogenous (community-chosen) rules change behaviour far more than imposed ones** (Dal Bó 2010; Ostrom Principle 3 & 7).
  - **Restorative accountability is *active*, not soft** (Zehr): punishment lets people passively "sustain suffering"; repair demands confronting harm — *harder* than a ban. ("Support without accountability = weakness; accountability without support = cruelty" — OJJDP.)
  - **The term "cancel culture" is politically toxic** (Fahey 2024; Pew: 38% never heard it; Public Leadership Institute: "reject the framing", describe specific harms instead).
  - **Loretta Ross's Five C's** (calling-in → on → out → cancelling → calling-it-off): callout is a *last resort*. Peer juries that start with repair and escalate proportionately implement this.
- CONFIRMS: 09 ("accountability without annihilation"; repair-before-exile is empirically sound, not soft); 10 ("community sets its own stakes by consent" = the evidence-backed compliance mechanism, not aspiration).
- CHALLENGES/CORRECTS:
  - 09: message "repair-first *increases* routes back", not "guarantees reconciliation" (keep the existing guard-rail).
  - 10 decay claim again needs the code check (done below).
- VERIFY FLAGS: Tyler's "7×" quantification appears in a Yale working paper — check exact peer-reviewed citation. Ostrom principles are inductive (800 cases), not RCTs.
- ACTION ITEMS: 09 → add Zehr "real accountability = understanding + repairing harm; harder than prison"; replace "repair before removal" with "repair as first resort, escalating fairly" (Ross + Ostrom). Both → **never use the phrase "cancel culture" in copy**; describe the experience ("instant unilateral exile, no due process, no route back"); lead with the positive frame "accountability without exile". 10 → footnote "by consent" to Tyler (voice = legitimacy) + Ostrom Principle 3.

## Section E digest — History verification → themes/03 + proof_points  ⚠ LOAD-BEARING

- KEY FINDINGS (with strength):
  - **Graeber & Wengrow** (*Dawn of Everything*, 2021) — SOLID for the general anti-linear-history argument; but named critics (Morris, Feinman, Smith, Appiah, Bell) flag cherry-picking. Cite for the *general* claim, NOT for specific archaeological facts without backup.
  - **Brehon law ran ~a millennium with no state police/prisons/public prosecutors** — SOLID. Suretyship (*ráth/naidm/aitire*) and distraint (*athgabál*) were real enforcement. Sources: Kelly (1988), Stacey (1994), Biggins (2024).
  - **Brehon law was hierarchical & inegalitarian** (slavery, unfree classes, honour-price by rank) — SOLID and must be cited alongside any Brehon claim.
  - **Brehon law was bottom-up** (norms → customs → good practice → codified) — SOLID (Biggins 2024). Safe to use (verify exact Biggins phrasing before quoting).
  - **Haudenosaunee consensus governance** — documented; but the "influenced the US Constitution" thesis is heavily contested (Tooker, Rakove, LaCroix). Only the weak version ("one influence among many") is safe.
  - **Athenian sortition** — real, but direct democracy covered only ~10–20% of the population (women/slaves/metics excluded). Structural, not incidental.
- **COURT-CASE VERDICT (the load-bearing answer):** There is **NO Brehon-specific modern court case.** Section E explicitly: no post-1922 Irish or UK case has successfully invoked Brehon law as binding/dispositive. (Non-binding only: Dáil Courts 1920–22 cited it; a 1933 fisheries case accepted Brehon evidence, "diluted" by 1948.) **Citable customary-law-in-courts precedents are all First Nations / Indigenous, NONE Brehon, NONE Irish:**
  - *Mabo v Queensland* [1992] HCA 23 — native title from Indigenous customary law survives colonisation.
  - *Ellis v R* [2022] NZSC — tikanga Māori is co-constitutive of common law.
  - *Tsilhqot'in Nation v BC* [2014] SCC 44 — first successful Aboriginal title declaration.
  - *Delgamuukw v BC* [1997] — oral histories on equal footing with written evidence.
- CONFIRMS (theme-03 safe to use): "humans have organised non-hierarchically by consent" (with caveat); "Brehon had no state/police/prisons" (cited); "rules were bottom-up" (Biggins); "customary law invoked in modern courts" — **YES but with the four non-Brehon cases above, never as Brehon-specific.**

  - Any Graeber/Wengrow specific claim → hedge with the critics.
  - Confirm messaging.yaml carries the same "not fully non-hierarchical / slavery" honesty as theme-03.
- VERIFY FLAGS: verify exact case citations + Biggins (2024) accessibility before linking; acquire Kelly/Stacey before quoting Brehon specifics.
